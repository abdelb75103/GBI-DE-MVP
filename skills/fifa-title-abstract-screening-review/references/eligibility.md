# Title/Abstract Eligibility Guidance

Use the same project eligibility frame as full-text screening, but apply it more leniently because title/abstract records may omit denominator, study design, sport subgroup, or outcome detail. The extraction end goal is football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, and mental-health/psychological-health surveillance data from current participating cohorts, with extractable numbers such as incidence, prevalence, burden, counts, rates, frequencies, exposure denominators, or comparable quantitative participant-health outcomes. The outcome must use consensus football injury/illness/all-health-problem definitions or be clearly mappable to football injury/health surveillance.

## Comprehensive Project Criteria

Eligible study types at full text include:

- Prospective cohort studies of current participants, including descriptive surveillance and risk-factor studies.
- Randomized controlled trials only when they contain eligible injury or illness epidemiology outcomes.
- Panel-design studies such as weekly player self-report using the OSTRC questionnaire; these are included but reported separately.
- Prospective, repeated, or surveillance self-report injury/health data. Self-report alone is not an exclusion.
- Direct football/soccer mental-health, psychological-health, injury-anxiety, health-problem, injury, or illness outcomes in current participants when they plausibly provide actual extractable prevalence, incidence, burden, counts, rates, frequencies, repeated measurements, validated symptom/risk scale results, or comparable participant-health numbers and are defined or mappable as participant-health surveillance outcomes.
- Any language.
- Regular seasonal competition, short-term tournaments such as the FIFA World Cup, and national or international club tournaments such as league cups and UEFA Champions League.
- Systematic reviews during screening for reference-list checks only when they are specifically relevant to football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health surveillance/epidemiology discovery. Do not retain irrelevant, broad mixed-topic, broad mixed-sport, prevention, RTP, rehabilitation, performance, mechanism, imaging, biomarker, headgear/protective-equipment, or risk-factor-only systematic/scoping reviews solely because they are reviews.

Some relevant studies may be reported as having a `retrospective` design even when the data were collected prospectively. This often refers to when the research question was conceived relative to prospectively collected surveillance data. At title/abstract stage, do not exclude solely for the word `retrospective` if prospective current-participant injury/illness surveillance may be present. Retrospective look-back data from former or retired footballers are excluded unless the record clearly shows the data were prospectively collected while they were current participants.

Eligible participants include competitive players of association football, futsal, beach soccer, and para football across men's and women's football, all age groups, all competitive levels, and all locations. Studies of football referees, assistant referees, and match officials are included but reported separately.

Full-text exclusions include:

- Sports other than FIFA-governed football disciplines or eligible para football.
- American football/NFL/gridiron, Canadian football, Australian rules/AFL, Gaelic football, rugby, and other non-association-football codes unless a football/soccer subgroup is clearly present.
- Non-competitive football such as recreational-only 5-a-side, football fitness, football as a medical intervention, and walking football.
- Former, retired, alumni, veteran, or post-career footballer datasets that retrospectively assess former-player health rather than prospectively collected current-participant surveillance.
- Narrative reviews, commentaries, editorials, protocols, and case reports.
- Retrospective cross-sectional or case-control studies unless the record may actually describe prospectively collected surveillance data.
- One-time retrospective injury-history recall or cross-sectional injury-history association studies, including self-reported previous injury history, when they do not plausibly describe prospective or repeated surveillance.
- Cohorts selected because participants already have a prior, recent, current, or surgically treated injury, such as ACL reconstruction/rupture, rerupture, acute tears, fractures, dislocations, or chronic pain, unless clearly nested inside prospective surveillance of a current participating cohort.
- Register-only studies such as ACL registers, hospital-record-only studies, national injury database studies, and retrospective public/league/database studies.
- Publicly available data sources such as Transfermarkt.com, PremierInjuries.com, public/media reports, and public league databases.
- Non-project injury/illness definitions such as injuries leading to hospitalization only.
- Studies only reporting injury numbers or proportions without exposure, rates, or enough information to calculate rates.
- Mortality or other catastrophic-outcome-only studies.
- Video-only public-video, broadcast-footage, match-footage, or video-analysis event-characteristic studies without an actual prospective injury dataset, even if they report potential events, match counts, exposure-adjusted rates, or per-1000-match-hour rates.

At title/abstract stage, apply those exclusions conservatively. If the title/abstract/citation has a plausible eligible football/soccer injury or illness surveillance signal but lacks enough detail to confirm denominators, rates, exact subgroup, or prospective collection, recommend `include` or `undecided` for full-text review rather than excluding prematurely. Do not exclude solely because the record says only `sport`, `athletes`, or a mixed-sport population without explicitly naming soccer/football; those records may still contain extractable soccer/football subgroup data. If no eligible football subgroup is visible but the record is not clearly a wrong sport/code or otherwise ineligible, recommend `undecided`.

Missing abstracts are not automatic `undecided`. If the title/citation alone clearly identifies an eligible football/soccer injury, illness, mental-health, referee, or surveillance record, make the title-only decision. If the title/citation alone clearly identifies American football or another wrong sport, a non-competitive football context, pure performance/rehabilitation/treatment/return-to-play without surveillance, or another non-eligible topic, recommend `exclude` with a criteria-based reason. Use `undecided` only when the title/citation is too thin to decide.

## Recommend Include

Recommend `include` when any of these are plausible from the title/abstract/citation:

- Football/soccer players, teams, match play, training, tournaments, academies, leagues, referees, assistant referees, or other match officials are studied.
- Injury, illness, concussion, epidemiology, incidence, prevalence, burden, surveillance, injury/illness counts, exposure denominators, or observed injury/illness risk-factor outcomes are reported or plausibly present.
- The abstract suggests player-level data that may contain a usable denominator after full-text review.
- The article is mixed-sport or unclear, but the title/abstract/citation provides a concrete signal that football-specific subgroup data may exist.
- Mixed sport alone is not an exclusion if football/soccer data may be extractable. If the abstract is unclear but soccer subgroup extraction is plausible, recommend `include` or `undecided`.
- The abstract is very sparse but still indicates a plausible football/soccer injury or illness record.
- Conference abstracts, supplement abstracts, or poster-style records when the supplied title/abstract/citation still gives a concrete football/soccer/futsal injury or illness epidemiology signal. These can be filtered later at full text if no usable full report exists.
- Relevant football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health reviews when they are specifically useful for surveillance/epidemiology reference-list checking. Do not include systematic/scoping reviews solely because they are reviews, and do not retain broad mixed-topic reviews where football/soccer is only one example or subgroup.
- Return-to-play or return-to-sport records only when they explicitly contain injury or illness surveillance data, injury/illness counts, incidence, burden, prevalence, exposure-denominator data, comparable quantitative epidemiological outcomes, or direct extractable mental-health outcomes. Do not include RTP criteria/practice/protocol, reinjury-risk, expert-judgement, clinical-management, performance, or rehabilitation records just because they concern injured footballers.
- Self-reported injury or health outcomes when the title/abstract suggests prospective, repeated, or surveillance collection, including weekly OSTRC-style reports.
- Referee or match-official records when they plausibly contain football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health surveillance numbers. These may later be tagged separately as referee records rather than primary player records.
- Mental-health, psychological-health, eating-disorder, distress, anxiety, depression, burnout, or injury-anxiety records in eligible current football/soccer participants when the title/abstract/citation plausibly contains actual extractable participant-health outcome data. Apply a slightly safer title/abstract threshold for these records because the mental-health evidence base is sparse, but do not include broad motivation, passion, wellness, sleep, menstrual-performance, quality-of-life, or general health-status records unless they report an extractable health endpoint defined or mappable as participant-health surveillance.

## Recommend Exclude

Recommend `exclude` only when clearly supported by the title/abstract/citation:

- Clearly a non-football/soccer sport or football code with no plausible football/soccer subgroup. Generic sport, athlete, or mixed-sport wording alone is not enough for this exclusion.
- Recreational-only, non-competitive context with no eligible football/soccer player, referee, or match-official population.
- Editorial, commentary, protocol, case report, biomechanical-only, performance-only, or intervention-only paper with no eligible injury/illness epidemiology outcome.
- Conference abstracts, supplement abstracts, posters, or narrative reviews only when they also lack a concrete football/soccer/futsal injury or illness epidemiology signal.
- Pure return-to-play, rehabilitation, treatment, prognosis, imaging, or performance records when the supplied title/abstract/citation clearly lacks injury/illness surveillance data or extractable injury/illness numbers.
- RTP criteria, RTP practice surveys, return-to-performance reviews, rehabilitation-exercise reviews, and post-surgical return-to-sport studies when they only describe clinical, functional, performance, or decision criteria rather than extractable injury/illness/mental-health surveillance outcomes.
- Studies that select only previously injured football/soccer players or patients and report post-injury functional outcomes, symptoms, rehabilitation response, treatment response, imaging findings, or return-to-function outcomes rather than surveillance incidence, prevalence, burden, rates, counts, frequency, or denominator data.
- Studies selected because participants have a prior, recent, current, or surgically treated injury, including ACL reconstruction/rupture, rerupture, acute tears, fractures, dislocations, and chronic pain cohorts, unless the injury subgroup is clearly nested in prospective surveillance of a current participating cohort.
- One-time cross-sectional or retrospective injury-history association records, even when the injury history is self-reported, when there is no prospective or repeated surveillance signal.
- Video-only public-video, broadcast-footage, match-footage, or video-analysis records of potential head injuries, suspected concussions, head collisions, headers, heading exposure, visible signs, medical-assessment behavior, injury mechanisms, or event characteristics when there is no actual prospective injury dataset or eligible health surveillance data.
- Downstream consequence records, such as imaging, white-matter hyperintensity, biomarker, neurocognitive, behavioral, or long-term sequelae studies after head impacts or injuries, when they do not report prevalence, incidence, burden, rates, exposure denominators, or surveillance of actual injuries/illnesses.
- Head-acceleration, head-kinematic, heading-protocol, cognitive-function, brain-structure/function, blood-biomarker, cardiac-troponin, and similar proxy/consequence records when they do not report actual injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health outcome surveillance.
- Economic, financial, return-on-investment, cost-benefit, implementation, policy/editorial, public-media-only, performance, body-composition, biomechanics, strength, range-of-motion, measurement, broad health-status, wellness, quality-of-life, imaging, biomarker, or physical-fitness records when they do not plausibly provide extractable project outcomes mapped to football injury/illness/health-problem surveillance.
- Pure attitude, knowledge, belief, awareness, perception, acceptability, or questionnaire-method studies, including mental-health-adjacent surveys, when they do not plausibly report direct injury, illness, mental-health prevalence, incidence, burden, counts, rates, frequency, or comparable participant-health outcome data.
- Public-source-only media, public database, national injury database, hospital/registry, or retrospective league/database dataset unless the supplied record clearly states that the underlying source was prospectively collected team/competition injury, illness, or health-problem surveillance from current participants.
- Clearly not human players.
- Clearly unrelated medical, social, or engineering topic.

## Recommend Include With Target Tag

Recommend `include` with `targetTag: "systematic_review"` when the record is a systematic review, scoping review, umbrella review, evidence synthesis, or meta-analysis relevant to football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health surveillance/epidemiology reference checking.

These records are included for Abdel's separate systematic-review handling, not as standard primary extraction papers.

## Recommend Undecided

Recommend `undecided` when the abstract is missing and the title/citation alone does not clearly support exclusion.

## Rationale Requirement

For every `exclude`, include:

- a short, criteria-based exclusion reason.

For every decision, leave quote/source fields empty.

For every systematic-review include, add `targetTag: "systematic_review"` and include `systematic_review` in `tags`.

For every `undecided`, explain that the abstract is missing or insufficient.
