# FIFA GBI Title/Abstract Runtime Criteria v1.10

Classify each supplied title/abstract record as `include`, `exclude`, or `undecided`.

Primary inclusion target:
- Plausible extractable football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health/psychological-health surveillance data under the full FIFA GBI inclusion criteria, from current participating player/referee cohorts or prospectively collected current-participant surveillance.
- Eligible football contexts include association football/soccer, futsal, beach soccer, eligible para-football, players, teams, leagues, academies, tournaments, referees, assistant referees, and other match officials.
- Eligible data signals include incidence, prevalence, burden, counts, rates, injury/illness frequency, exposure denominators, participant/time denominators, epidemiology, surveillance tables, health-problem surveillance, concussion surveillance, and mental-health surveillance. The health issue/problem must be defined with consensus football injury/illness/all-health-problem definitions or be clearly mappable to football injury/health surveillance outcomes such as time-loss, medical-attention, all-complaints/OSTRC-style, or comparable player/referee health surveillance.
- Eligible study designs at full text include prospective cohort studies of current participants, descriptive prospective surveillance, prospective risk-factor studies, randomized controlled trials with eligible prospective injury/illness epidemiology outcomes, and panel designs such as weekly OSTRC self-report. Short-term tournaments, seasonal competition, league cups, UEFA Champions League, FIFA World Cup, national and international club tournaments, all languages, men's and women's football, all age groups, all competitive levels, and all locations are eligible.
- Some relevant papers may describe the analysis as retrospective even when the injury/illness data were collected prospectively. Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected current-participant surveillance data is allowed only when the supplied record clearly shows the underlying source was prospectively collected team/competition injury, illness, or health-problem surveillance from current participants. Hospital-only, registry-only, national injury database, public-source, media-source, public-database, and retrospective database studies are excluded even when they report incidence, prevalence, epidemiology, or injury rates.
- Self-reported injury or health data can be eligible when collected prospectively, repeatedly, or as surveillance, including weekly OSTRC-style player reports. Do not exclude just because the data are self-reported.
- Ultimate screening purpose: retain papers that may yield extractable project outcomes, including injury, illness, health-problem, and mental-health/psychological-health prevalence, incidence, burden, counts, rates, frequencies, exposure denominators, or comparable surveillance data. Exclude records that are only about downstream consequences, economics, policy, performance, biomechanics, body composition, treatment response, or measurement when they do not plausibly report those extractable outcomes.

Mental-health and psychological-health records:
- Use slightly more leeway for football/soccer mental-health, psychological-health, and injury-anxiety records because these data are sparse, but still require actual extractable participant-health data. Include only when the title/abstract plausibly reports prevalence, incidence, burden, counts, rates, frequencies, repeated measurements, validated symptom/risk scale results, or comparable participant-health data that can be extracted for the review.
- Do not include broad motivation, passion, wellness, sleep, menstrual-performance, quality-of-life, body-composition, imaging, biomarker, performance, or general health-status records unless the supplied metadata shows an actual extractable injury, illness, health-problem, mental-health, eating-disorder, distress, anxiety, depression, burnout, or injury-anxiety endpoint that is clearly mappable to football health surveillance.
- Exclude pure attitude, knowledge, belief, awareness, perception, satisfaction, acceptability, or questionnaire-method records when they do not plausibly measure injury, illness, mental-health prevalence, incidence, burden, rates, or comparable participant-health outcomes. Descriptive survey frequencies about satisfaction, awareness, preferences, or perceptions are not enough.

Referees and match officials:
- Include plausible football/soccer referee, assistant referee, or match-official injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health surveillance records.
- Add tag `referee` for these records.

Safe title/abstract rule:
- If a plausible football/soccer injury or illness surveillance signal is present in a current participating cohort but denominator, exact sport subgroup, or extractable numbers are unclear, choose `include` or `undecided` so it reaches full-text review. This leniency does not apply to video-only event/proxy studies, broad mixed-topic systematic reviews, or rehab/RTP/prevention/education/attitude records without actual extractable epidemiology or participant-health outcomes.
- Missing abstracts are not automatic `undecided`. If the title/citation alone clearly supports an eligible football/soccer injury, illness, mental-health, referee, or surveillance decision, decide from the title/citation. If the title/citation alone clearly supports exclusion, such as American football/wrong sport, non-competitive football, pure performance/rehabilitation/treatment/return-to-play without surveillance, or another non-eligible topic, choose `exclude` with a criteria-based reason.
- Use `undecided` mainly for missing or too-thin abstracts where the title/citation is not decisive.
- Do not exclude solely because the supplied title/abstract says only `sport`, `athletes`, or a mixed-sport population without explicitly naming soccer/football. Generic or mixed-sport records may contain extractable soccer/football subgroup data. If no eligible football subgroup is visible but the record is not clearly a wrong sport/code or otherwise ineligible, choose `undecided`.
- Do not apply full-text denominator exclusions too early. If a football/soccer/futsal/beach/para-football record reports injury cases, injury data, injury counts, player injuries, or a specific injury series in current eligible players/referees, choose `include` or `undecided` unless it is clearly only a case report, selected prior-injury cohort, treatment, surgery, imaging, or rehabilitation paper with no extractable epidemiology value.
- For mixed-sport primary records and para-sport records, choose `include` or `undecided` when the supplied metadata, keywords, title, or abstract contains a football/soccer/futsal/beach/para-football signal that may support subgroup extraction. Mixed sport alone is not an exclusion if soccer data may be extractable; if unsure, let it proceed.
- Systematic/scoping reviews are not automatically included. Retain them only when they are specifically relevant to football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health surveillance/epidemiology reference checking. Exclude broad mixed-topic or mixed-sport reviews where football/soccer is only one example or subgroup, and exclude reviews focused on prevention exercises, headgear/protective equipment, RTP criteria, rehabilitation exercises, performance tests, risk factors without epidemiology, head-acceleration proxies, biomarkers, imaging, mechanisms, kinematics, or other non-extractable topics.

Exclude when clearly unsupported:
- Clearly wrong sport or football code with no football/soccer/futsal/beach/para-football subgroup. Generic sport, athlete, or mixed-sport wording is not enough for this exclusion.
- In the USA and Canada, `football` often means American football; exclude NFL, gridiron, Canadian football, Australian rules/AFL, Gaelic football, rugby, and other non-FIFA football codes unless a football/soccer subgroup is clearly present.
- Non-competitive football contexts such as recreational-only 5-a-side, football fitness, football as a medical intervention, and walking football.
- Former, retired, alumni, veteran, or post-career footballer datasets that look back retrospectively or assess long-term former-player health, unless the supplied record clearly indicates prospectively collected surveillance from when participants were current players/referees.
- Pure return-to-play, return-to-sport, rehabilitation, treatment, prognosis, imaging, surgery, performance, physiology, biomechanics, measurement, or intervention records with no plausible injury/illness surveillance data or extractable injury/illness numbers.
- RTP criteria, return-to-performance, RTP practice surveys, rehabilitation-exercise reviews, post-surgical return-to-sport studies, and clinical decision-making papers are excluded unless they plausibly report actual injury/illness/health-problem/mental-health surveillance outcomes.
- Studies that select only already-injured football/soccer players or patients and report post-injury functional outcomes, symptoms, rehabilitation response, treatment response, imaging findings, or return-to-function outcomes, with no injury/illness surveillance denominator or extractable incidence, prevalence, burden, frequency, rate, or count data.
- Studies that select players because they have a prior, recent, current, or surgically treated injury, such as ACL reconstruction/rupture, rerupture, acute tears, fractures, dislocations, or chronic pain cohorts, are excluded unless they are clearly nested in prospective surveillance of a current participating cohort.
- One-time cross-sectional or retrospective injury-history association studies, including self-reported previous injury or injury-history recall, when they lack prospective or repeated surveillance, incidence, prevalence, burden, rate, or exposure-denominator data.
- Video-only public-video, broadcast-footage, match-footage, or video-analysis event-characteristic records without an actual prospective injury dataset are excluded even if they report match counts, potential event counts, exposure-adjusted rates, or per-1000-match-hour rates. This includes potential head injuries, suspected concussions, head collisions, headers, heading exposure, visible signs, medical-assessment behavior, and injury-mechanism/event-characteristic studies based only on footage review.
- Pure qualitative, interview, satisfaction, survey, or questionnaire studies of attitudes, knowledge, beliefs, awareness, perceptions, acceptability, preferences, educational interventions, after-care/signposting, injury-prevention opinions, or RTP/rehab opinions are excluded unless they report direct extractable mental-health outcomes. Descriptive frequencies for these opinions or satisfaction outcomes are not eligible epidemiology.
- Generic risk-factor, association, prevention, or prediction papers with no injury/illness outcome counts, rates, burden, prevalence, incidence, frequency, denominator, or surveillance signal.
- Downstream consequence studies, including imaging, white-matter hyperintensity, biomarker, neurocognitive, behavioral, or long-term sequelae studies of prior head impacts or injuries, when they do not report prevalence, incidence, burden, rates, exposure denominators, or surveillance of actual injuries/illnesses.
- Head acceleration, head kinematics, heading exposure, brain structure/function, cognitive function, cardiac troponin, blood biomarkers, and similar proxy/consequence outcomes are not enough by themselves; exclude them unless actual project outcome surveillance is reported.
- Economic, cost-benefit, return-on-investment, implementation, or financial modeling studies when they are not standalone sources of extractable injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health outcome data.
- Performance, body-composition, biomechanics, strength, range-of-motion, measurement, or physical-fitness studies when they do not report extractable project outcomes.
- Broad health-status, wellness, motivation, passion, affect, quality-of-life, imaging, biomarker, or physical examination records when the reported issue is not defined by consensus football injury/illness/all-health-problem definitions and is not mappable to injury/illness/health-problem surveillance.
- Protocols, narrative reviews, editorials, commentaries, case reports, cross-sectional/case-control studies, non-human studies, register-only studies, hospital-record-only studies, national injury database studies, retrospective public/league/database studies, public-media-only datasets such as Transfermarkt or PremierInjuries, mortality/catastrophic-outcome-only studies, and unrelated medical/social/engineering topics.
- Studies using non-project injury/illness definitions such as hospitalisation-only injury definitions.
- At full text, studies only reporting injury numbers or proportions without exposure, rates, or enough information to calculate rates are excluded. At title/abstract stage, do not exclude plausible football/soccer injury-count or injury-series records solely because the denominator is not visible in the abstract.
- Do not treat match count alone as eligible exposure for retrospective video-analysis records when the record only describes potential events or event characteristics rather than player/referee health-problem surveillance.
- Relevant systematic reviews are included in screening for reference-list checks and should be tagged `systematic_review`; narrative reviews and irrelevant systematic/scoping reviews are excluded unless they are specifically football/soccer injury, illness, health-problem, or mental-health epidemiology/surveillance reviews, not broad mixed-topic reviews.

Return-to-play caveat:
- Do not include pure RTP/RTS papers just because they concern injured football/soccer players.
- Include only if the title/abstract explicitly reports injury or illness surveillance data, injury incidence, burden, prevalence, rates, exposure denominators, or comparable extractable epidemiological data, or direct extractable mental-health outcomes. Do not include RTP, rehabilitation, prevention, or return-to-performance papers for reinjury risk, clinical management, criteria, expert judgement, performance outcomes, or prevention strategy unless the supplied record clearly contains usable epidemiological or participant-health outcome data.
- Exclude surgical, rehabilitation, treatment, clinical-outcome, complication-rate, radiographic-healing, and functional-outcome RTP cohorts when participants were selected because they already had the injury and no eligible surveillance denominator or injury/illness epidemiology data are present.

Compact output:
- Return JSON only as an array.
- Shape: `[{"id":"record-id","d":"include|exclude|undecided","r":"reason_code","c":0.0,"t":["optional_tag"],"n":"short optional note"}]`
- Do not return quote or source-location fields.
