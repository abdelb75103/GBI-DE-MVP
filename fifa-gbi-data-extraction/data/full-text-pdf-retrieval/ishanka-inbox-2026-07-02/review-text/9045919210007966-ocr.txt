ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND: Although hamstring strain injuries (HSI) are commoniy reported injuries in football, there are limited
studies on the incidence of HSI and its intrinsic factors in Malaysian football settings. The aim of this study was to ex-
amine the incidence of HSI among Malaysian footballers and study intrinsic risk factors (hamstring strength, intra and
interlimb deficits) associated with the risk of HSI.
METHODS: This is a cohort study of 81 Malaysian professional footballers in a single renowned club. This study was
conducted throughout 2023 Malaysian Football League. Preseason medical assessments include demographic data, an-

known. Football skills involve sprinting, acceler-
ation and deceleration, rapid change in direction,
and jumping. Such skills predispose footballers
to an increased risk of developing hamstring
injury.4, s During high-speed running, the ham-
string muscle plays a major role by contracting
eccentrically to slow down knee extension dur-
ing the terminal swing phase.6 Thus, investment
in a hamstring injury prevention program and

Materials and methods
Study design
This was a prospective cohort study of 81 Ma-
laysian professional footballers, from a single
renowned football club.
Preseason assessments
The preseason assessments were performed at
the football club itself. Footballers were asked to
rest for 1 day before medical assessments. The
demographic information gathered included age,
gender, position played, prior history of ham-
string injury, and dominant leg using a standard-
ized registry. Football club name, ID number,
and name were gathered but not included in the
analysis. InBody 370 Body Composition Analy-
sis (InBody, Amsterdam, the Netherlands) was
used for anthropometric measurements and as-
sessments was done by a sports medicine train-
ee. Before the test, footballers were instructed
to fast for six hours. The standardized registry
contained information on height, weight, Body
Mass Index (BMI), body fat mass, and skeletal
muscle mass. Assessment of muscle strength was
preceded by a 10-15 min warm-up. Every foot-
baller received sufficient familiarization prior to
testing. For each variable, three measurements
were acquired and averaged. Using a TECH
Commander Powertrack Muscle Dynamometer
(JTECH Medical Industries, Inc., Midvale, UT,
USA) for manual muscle testing, one identical
examiner (examiner 1) assessed isometric torque
in the hamstrings and quadriceps. The techniques
of the manual muscle testing are outlined in Ta-
ble I. Hand held dynamometer muscle testing
was used as it has: 1) high correlation between
hip, knee, and ankle strength compared with iso-
kinetic; 2) is less expensive; and 3) is practicable
in the clinic and office. 11, 12
Injury surveillance
Data collection was conducted based on the
method proposed by the Injury Consensus Group,
which was established under the Fédération In-
ternationale de Football Association Medical
Assessment and Research Centre (F-MARC). 13
All injuries during training and competition re-
ported by the footballers to either team physician
and/or team physiotherapist, will be recorded,

Statistical analysis

future. There is a possibility of under-reported,
self-treated minor hamstring strain among U23,
and U20, as there were no hamstring injuries re-
ported in that group.
Conclusions
The incidence of HSI is comparable to the Eu-
ropean, and Australian populations, indicating
that the injury prevention program in Malaysia
is up to par with the international standard. Few
risk factors of HSI have been identified among
Malaysian footballers; previous hamstring in-
jury, low H:Q ratio, and intra-limb hamstring
asymmetry >15%. It is noteworthy that isomet-
ric hamstring strength is as important as eccen-
tric hamstring strength, in predicting the risk
of future HSI. Thus, focusing on both eccentric
and isometric hamstring strengthening in injury
prevention program would be helpful to further
reduce the risk. We recommend for further re-
search on isometric hamstring strengthening ex-
ercises, as part of an injury prevention program
in the future.
