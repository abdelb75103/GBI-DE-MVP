S5553

**Title:** Erstellung eines Verletzungsregisters im Profifusball - Studiendesign und Perspektiven

**Recommendation:** Include

**Criteria:** I-DES-01, I-POP-01, I-SET-01, I-OUT-01, I-DEF-01, I-DEN-01

**Study design:** Prospective national registry study

**Data collection timing:** Men's registry introduced in 2022/2023 and women's registry in 2023/2024, with monthly team-medical-staff reporting and longitudinal follow-up.

**Population / discipline / context:** Professional male and female football players in the German first and second Bundesliga (1,514 players from 54 teams in the first season).; Association football; German professional men's and women's first and second Bundesliga league competitions and training.

**Data source:** Prospective REDCap registries completed by team medical staff, with official match statistics for exposure data.

**Outcome / definition:** Time-loss injuries, illnesses, injury incidence and return-to-play duration.; All time-loss injuries and illnesses reported with standardised electronic forms, adapted from established football and IOC surveillance protocols; return to play is medical clearance for full team training and match play.

**Denominator:** published_time_rate; Published injury incidence per 1,000 hours of match exposure, including 3.16 for male ankle sprain and 2.56 for female ankle sprain.

**Derivation basis:**

**Evidence:** Methods Two prospective registries were introduced in the first and second divisions of German professional football (men: 2022/2023, women: 2023/2024). Team medical staff reported all time-loss injuries and illnesses using a standardised, pseudonymised protocol on an online platform. | Match exposure data were obtained directly from official match statistics for all players. Injury and illness match incidence rates were calculated per 1000 hours of match exposure. | The most frequent injury in male professional football in Germany was ankle sprains with an incidence of 3.16 per 1000 hours match exposure and a mean time-loss of 7.3 days. | During the first season, 1185 time-loss events were recorded (men: 503 injuries, 176 illnesses; women: 362 injuries, 144 illnesses).
